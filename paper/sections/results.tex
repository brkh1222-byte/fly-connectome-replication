\subsection*{A float32 batched implementation reproduces the Brian2 model}
The PyTorch implementation matched Brian2 within seed noise on FlyWire v783 in all three
validation experiments (Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}; \texttt{results/gpu\_validation.md}).
Per-neuron rate correlations with Brian2 were 0.9994--0.9997, the same as between two
independent Brian2 runs (0.9995--0.9997). Readouts agreed: sugar$\to$MN9 62.7 vs 61.4\,Hz,
sugar plus bitter 2.9 vs 3.5\,Hz, and JO-C/E$\to$aBN1 26.2 vs 25.9\,Hz (PyTorch vs Brian2;
95\% confidence intervals overlap). \todo{CUDA row from results/gpu\_validation\_cuda.md.}
With Shiu's own neuron lists, the Brian2 model reproduced the published behaviour on both
FlyWire versions (Fig.~\ref{fig:repro}; \texttt{results/repro\_shiu.csv}). Sugar GRN input at
100\,Hz drove MN9 at 67.7\,Hz in v630 and 61.3\,Hz in v783. Bitter input at 100\,Hz reduced
the v783 response to 3.6\,Hz. Water GRNs did not reach MN9 at 100\,Hz but did at 200\,Hz
(31.7\,Hz). JO-C/E input at 160\,Hz drove aBN1 at 55.6\,Hz, against 1.3\,Hz for JO-F.

\subsection*{The three connectomes differ about four-fold in synapse density}
After standardisation the brain subgraphs held 138{,}639 (FlyWire), 126{,}389 (BANC) and
144{,}717 (male CNS) neurons, with 54.5, 23.3 and 101.1 million synapses
(\texttt{results/dataset\_summary.csv}). 6{,}531 cell types were present in all three. Per
shared cell type, the median total input per neuron relative to FlyWire was 0.45 in BANC
(interquartile range 0.33--0.62) and 1.76 in the male CNS (1.40--2.27)
(\texttt{results/type\_input\_comparison.csv}). The BANC deficit agrees with the 2.1-fold
figure of \citep{santos2026}. Because $\Wsyn$ is a per-synapse weight tuned on FlyWire, the
unchanged model is under-driven in BANC and over-driven in the male CNS. (Fig.~\ref{fig:datasets}).

\subsection*{Replication of the validated predictions}
\textbf{All results in the remaining sections are preliminary.} They come from laptop-scale
runs: one set of 30 trials per network, three degraded copies and three shuffles per
connectome. The full grid (Methods) will replace them. \todo{replace with full-grid numbers}

Table~\ref{tab:rep} lists the readouts (\texttt{results/replication\_table.csv}). With
type-defined stimuli at 150\,Hz, FlyWire reproduced sugar$\to$MN9 (72.1\,Hz), bitter
suppression (85\%) and JO-C/E$\to$aBN1 (34.1\,Hz, against 0.1\,Hz for JO-F) for left-side
stimulation. Water GRNs (LB3a alone) did not reach MN9 at 150\,Hz in FlyWire, consistent with
the 200\,Hz needed in the Brian2 reproduction, so water$\to$MN9 cannot be tested at this rate.
Right-side sugar stimulation in FlyWire recruited about 8{,}500 neurons and left MN9 at
1.4\,Hz. This is the brain-wide response that \citep{santos2026} traced to one mis-signed
neuron in the model file.

\textbf{BANC.} With raw synapse counts (N0), BANC lost every activation prediction: MN9 and
aBN1 stayed at 0\,Hz for all stimuli, in agreement with \citep{santos2026} and with the weak
response in \citep{brainlab2026}. Global density scaling (N1, a factor of 2.16) did not
restore sugar$\to$MN9 (0\,Hz left, 2.6\,Hz right). It restored JO-C/E$\to$aBN1 on the left
(22.5\,Hz), but in a brain-wide active state of about 13{,}000 neurons, and right sugar and
right water stimulation also entered such states.

\textbf{Male CNS.} With raw counts the male CNS, which has 1.76 times FlyWire's input per
cell type, reproduced sugar$\to$MN9 (72.6 and 71.1\,Hz), bitter suppression and
JO-C/E$\to$aBN1 (98.9 and 62.9\,Hz), but every protocol put more than 9{,}000 neurons into
activity. Under N1 (a factor of 0.61) activity was sparse for most protocols.
JO-C/E$\to$aBN1 (84.7 and 56.4\,Hz) and bitter suppression held on both sides;
sugar$\to$MN9 held on the right (30.5\,Hz) but fell to 5.7\,Hz on the left, below the 20\%
criterion. Bitter stimulation still recruited about 9{,}000 neurons.

In degree-preserving shuffles of each connectome, MN9 and aBN1 stayed at 0\,Hz in all
protocols (3 shuffles per connectome; \texttt{results/control\_readouts.csv}).

\subsection*{An agreement ladder across three connectomes}
Cell-type response profiles agreed weakly between connectomes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}; \texttt{results/agreement\_summary.csv}). Under N1 the median
Spearman $\rho$ over protocols and sides was 0.21 for FlyWire--BANC, 0.10 for FlyWire--male
CNS and 0.03 for BANC--male CNS; under N0 the values were 0.20, 0.03 and 0.04. The
FlyWire--BANC value is close to the $\rho\le0.32$ of \citep{santos2026}. The male CNS did
not agree with FlyWire better than BANC did. Hemisphere agreement was 0.35 in FlyWire, 0.52
(N1) to 0.77 (N0) in the male CNS, and 0.07 (N1) to 0.32 (N0) in BANC; \citep{santos2026}
reported 0.54 and 0.47. Against degree-preserving shuffles, profiles were anticorrelated
(median $\rho$ $-0.42$ for FlyWire, $-0.42$ for BANC, $-0.12$ for the male CNS, N1), so the
between-connectome values sit above this floor.

Brain-wide active states dominate these numbers. In 11 of the 36 base experiments under N1
more than 2\% of neurons were active (\texttt{results/activity\_table.csv}), and a pair of
profiles disagrees almost completely when only one network is in that state. Restricting to
pairs in which neither network was in such a state (an exploratory analysis defined after
seeing the data) gave medians of 0.21 (FlyWire--BANC), 0.18 (FlyWire--male CNS) and 0.48
(FlyWire hemispheres) under N1.

\subsection*{Does reconstruction completeness explain the FlyWire--BANC gap?}
Thinning FlyWire to BANC's per-type input (three copies) reproduced BANC's loss of the
feeding prediction under raw weights: sugar$\to$MN9 fell from 72.1\,Hz to 0\,Hz in all
three copies, as in BANC. JO-C/E$\to$aBN1 fell from 34.1 to 14.3\,Hz, where BANC gave
0\,Hz (\texttt{results/control\_readouts.csv}). So BANC's lower synapse density is by itself
enough to abolish sugar$\to$MN9 in this model.

Two observations show that density is not the whole explanation. First, global rescaling
(N1) restored sugar$\to$MN9 in degraded FlyWire (69.1\,Hz; range 63.0--72.4) but not in BANC
(0\,Hz). Second, degraded FlyWire stayed much closer to FlyWire than BANC was. Under N0 the
median $\rho$ between FlyWire and its degraded copies was 0.55, and the observed
FlyWire--BANC $\rho$ fell below the range of the degraded copies in all nine protocol--side
combinations that could be evaluated (\texttt{results/completeness\_test.csv}); under N1 it
fell below in seven of nine. With three copies the range is a minimum--maximum, not a 95\%
interval. Degraded FlyWire under N1 also entered brain-wide activity for JO-C/E stimulation
(about 10{,}000 active neurons), as BANC did.

The preliminary verdict is therefore \emph{partly explained}: uniform per-type loss of
synapses accounts for the failure of the headline prediction in BANC as released, but not
for the failure after density rescaling, nor for most of the profile disagreement.
